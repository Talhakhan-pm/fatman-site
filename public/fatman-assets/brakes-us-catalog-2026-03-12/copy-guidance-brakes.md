# Brakes Product-Card Copy Guidance

## Goal
Make the brake listings sound like believable U.S. replacement-parts inventory. Keep the language specific to the visible brake archetype, use the image as support rather than proof of exact fitment, and avoid race-shop hype unless the real product data truly supports it.

- ceramic front or rear pad sets

## Brake Copy Rules
- Sound like a smart parts counterperson, not a performance influencer.
- Avoid: beast, monster, ultimate stopping power, race-proven, track weapon, insane bite.
- Prefer: OE-style, loaded assembly, vented rotor, slotted rotor, low-dust ceramic, direct-fit hose, braided stainless, hydraulic pressure, integrated parking brake, corrosion-resistant hat coating.
- Do not invent exact rotor diameter, piston count, bore size, DOT approval wording beyond what real product data states, fitment ranges, or brand-specific claims.

## Anti-Repetition Detail Bank
Rotate these details instead of repeating the same sentence structure:
- noise-dampening shims
- chamfered friction material
- coated rotor hat
- vented disc construction
- slotted street-performance surface
- installed slider pins
- integrated parking-brake lever
- crimped end fittings
- protective braided sheathing
- factory-style hydraulic port layout

## Practical Integration Advice
- Keep grid-card titles short and neutral.
- Let the image suggest the archetype; let real product data supply the exact specs.
- Use the rotor images broadly, but do not force the slotted rotor asset onto standard OE rotor listings.
- Use the master-cylinder and hose images to break up a rotor-heavy brakes category so the catalog feels more real and less copy-pasted.
